### Short Summary – *Ten Simple Rules for Reading a Scientific Paper*

The paper **“Ten Simple Rules for Reading a Scientific Paper”** explains how to read research papers in a smarter and more organised way. The main idea is that we don't have to read every paper from the first page to the last. We should first understand what the research is about and then decide how deeply we need to read it.

I would first read the **abstract** to check if the paper is actually relevant to me. It gives a quick idea about the research question, approach and main findings. If it seems useful, I can then go through the **introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusion and citations**. Each section helps answer a different part of the research. The introduction explains the problem, the methods show how it was studied, the results give the findings, and the discussion and conclusion explain what those findings mean. The citations can also help me find other useful papers.

What I found most useful was the idea of **asking questions while reading** instead of just highlighting everything. I can ask what the researchers wanted to find out, why they used a certain method, what the results show and whether their conclusion is supported by the evidence.

Next time I read a research paper, I will **start with the abstract to check its relevance**, understand the main research question, and then focus more on the sections that actually matter to me. This paper made me realise that reading research is not about understanding every word immediately, but about **understanding the research and thinking critically about what it is saying**.
